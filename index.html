About Me

I am a Robotics Software Engineer at Enchanted Tools in Paris, where I work on navigation and autonomy for Mirokaï robots. I am especially interested in how robots perceive people, understand the structure of a social scene, and move naturally and safely around humans.

I like working across the full robotics stack, from sensing and perception to navigation, embedded systems, integration, and validation on real robots. Most of my work is built around C++, Python, ROS 2, NVIDIA Jetson platforms, and the practical problems that appear when algorithms leave simulation and have to run reliably on hardware.

Alongside my work, I am completing an MSc in Embedded Systems Engineering at ENSTA - Institut Polytechnique de Paris. My current projects explore social navigation with vision-language models, humanoid robotics, and autonomous competition robots.

What I Work On

Social navigation on Mirokaï

At Enchanted Tools, I work on the navigation R&D stack used by Mirokaï robots. I have integrated a complete social-navigation pipeline combining 3D human perception, body-orientation estimation, social-formation tracking, and motion-aware proxemic cost modeling so that navigation decisions account for people rather than treating them as ordinary obstacles.

I also developed a 360-degree near-field perception pipeline that fuses nine time-of-flight sensors with an RGB-D camera for local costmaps and collision monitoring, and I built and validated Open-RMF fleet orchestration across multiple physical Mirokaï robots.

Embodied AI for robot navigation

I am interested in using vision-language models as a high-level reasoning layer for navigation without putting them directly in the low-level control loop. My current VLM2Nav work studies how temporal visual observations can be converted into structured social decisions that are then executed by a conventional ROS 2 navigation stack.

Building complete robotic systems

Beyond navigation, I enjoy building systems end to end: embedded firmware, CAN communication, state estimation, perception, planning, control, simulation, testing, and bring-up. I tend to use projects and competitions as a way to validate ideas against real hardware rather than stopping at isolated algorithms.

Selected Projects

VLM2Nav

Vision-Language Models for Social Robot Navigation

2026 - Present

A ROS 2 and Gazebo testbed for studying social navigation with Qwen3-VL served through vLLM on Jetson AGX Orin. Temporal RGB observations are translated into structured social decisions, while Nav2 and MPPI remain responsible for motion generation and low-level control. HuNavSim provides reactive pedestrians and a reproducible nine-scenario benchmark.

ROS 2 · Nav2 · MPPI · Qwen3-VL · vLLM · Jetson AGX Orin · HuNavSim

PHARE

Autonomous Humanoid Research and Experimentation Platform

2026 - Present

I co-founded PHARE and lead software and embedded systems for a modular wheeled-biped humanoid research platform at ENSTA. The project covers ROS 2, embedded firmware and electronics, CAN architecture, state estimation, optimal control, reinforcement learning, simulation, and the transition from digital models to a physical robot.

RoboCup MSL

Robot Club Toulon · Goalkeeper Robot Lead

2022 - Present

I develop autonomous goalkeeper behaviors in C#, including stereo-camera 3D ball reconstruction, ballistic trajectory prediction, reactive positioning, and shot interception with a two-axis arm. The team finished world runner-up at RoboCup 2026 in Incheon, South Korea.

FIPATECH

French Robotics Cup · Founder, Team Leader & Software Lead

2024 - 2026

I founded and led the team around an autonomous holonomic robot built with ROS 2, Nav2, MPPI, multi-sensor localization, and online task planning. We took the system from simulation through integration and competition, finishing 17th out of 89 teams in the Senior category in 2026.

Background

Before joining the Navigation R&D team at Enchanted Tools, I worked there as a Test and Integration Technician Intern in 2024. I designed and iterated a 3D-printed charging interface for autonomous docking and characterized seven pairs of robotic hands through endurance, precision, motion-capture, and field tests.

I study embedded systems engineering at ENSTA through apprenticeship after completing a BSc in Electrical Engineering and Industrial Computing at the University of Toulon. I am also President of the ENSTA Robotics Club, a maintainer of Awesome French Robotics, and a C++ contributor to PaxOS.
